**Figure 1. Study area: eight Odisha districts partitioned into five coastal treatment and three inland control units, shown with International Best Track Archive for Climate Stewardship (IBTrACS) best-track paths for the three pre-Kharif cyclones.**
District polygons are drawn directly from the geoBoundaries Open IND (India, ISO 3166-1 alpha-3) ADM2 (Administrative Level 2) release (Runfola et al., 2020; build dated 12 December 2023; Open Database License version 1.0, ODbL-1.0), which is harmonised against the official lgdirectory.gov.in Indian administrative roster and is functionally equivalent at this scale to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) Global Administrative Unit Layers (GAUL) Level-2 boundaries (FAO, 2015) used in the underlying Google Earth Engine (GEE) processing. The five coastal treatment districts (Baleshwar, Bhadrak, Kendrapara, Jagatsinghpur, Puri) are filled orange and the three inland control districts (Angul, Dhenkanal, Cuttack) are filled blue. The basemap (ocean, land, coastline) is rendered from Natural Earth 10 m vector layers (Patterson and Kelso, 2024) and the faint orange outline shows the Natural Earth 10 m Odisha state polygon for reference. Coloured curves are the IBTrACS version 04r01 best-track positions (Knapp et al., 2010) drawn from the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) latitude/longitude columns, truncated from the first cyclone-strength fix in the Bay of Bengal to approximately 12 hours after landfall. Coloured stars mark the India Meteorological Department (IMD)-documented landfall sites, taken from the official RSMC New Delhi post-cyclone reports rather than the nearest IBTrACS 3-hourly fix (which can fall up to ~80 km away from the actual coast-crossing point because IBTrACS records storm-centre positions every 3 hours): Cyclone Fani made landfall near Puri at approximately 02:30 UTC on 03 May 2019 (~19.80°N / 85.83°E), with WMO peak intensity 115 knots, kt; IMD classification: Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm, ESCS. Cyclone Amphan made landfall near Bakkhali in the Sundarbans delta, West Bengal, at approximately 09:00 UTC on 20 May 2020 (~21.60°N / 88.25°E), with WMO peak intensity 130 kt; IMD classification: Super Cyclonic Storm, SuCS. Cyclone Yaas made landfall near Bahanaga/Dhamra Port on the Bhadrak–Baleshwar coast at approximately 05:30 UTC on 26 May 2021 (~21.42°N / 86.94°E), with WMO peak intensity 75 kt; IMD classification: Very Severe Cyclonic Storm, VSCS. Dotted circles delineate the 50-km buffer around each IMD landfall site (50 km computed in the India Albers Equal Area projection, European Petroleum Survey Group code EPSG:7755, then re-projected back to geographic coordinates). Inset (bottom right): location of the Odisha study window within India (red box delineates the combined 8-district extent — district bounds approximately 84.27–87.49°E, 19.47–21.98°N — drawn with approximately 0.2° padding on each side for visibility), rendered at Natural Earth 50 m resolution with the Odisha state polygon highlighted in orange. Underlying quantitative analysis uses the FAO GAUL Level-2 district polygons (FAO, 2015) and the European Space Agency (ESA) WorldCover version 200 (v200) cropland mask (Zanaga et al., 2022) in GEE; this overview map is a navigational aid only, and quantitative panel statistics are reported in Tables 2–3 and Figure 7.

**Figure 2. Headline QC demonstration: SOS, POS, and EOS distributions before (v1.0.2, top row) and after (v2.0, bottom row) the quality-control framework.**
Top row — pre-QC district-year aggregates from the v1.0.2 pipeline (n = 64 district-year cells for SOS and POS; n = 44 for EOS, after 20 boundary-censored cells are excluded). Two boundary spikes are visually apparent: 72.7% of EOS district-year medians cluster at DOY 349 (the centre of the last full dekadal bin before the year-end fitting-window terminus at DOY 365, annotated in the EOS panel) and 65.6% of POS district-year medians cluster at DOY 288 (a dekadal-bin concentration at the canopy peak). The SOS distribution carries a milder mode-share of 20.3% at DOY 196. Bottom row — post-QC cell-level fits from the v2.0 pipeline (n = 1,223 QC-passing cell-years from 436 cells across 8 districts and 6 years), shown at native cell-level granularity to expose the full post-QC distribution shape rather than collapsing back to 48 district-year medians. The cell-level mode-shares (2.0% SOS, 4.5% POS, 4.2% EOS) and the corresponding district-year-aggregated post-QC mode-shares (8.3% SOS, 8.3% POS, 8.3% EOS, reported in Table 2) both fall well below the Gate A threshold of 0.20. The distributions broaden to reflect genuine biological variability in transplanting, heading, and harvest timing across districts, years, and cultivars. Mode-share annotations (red box, top row; blue box, bottom row) report the empirical mode-share at the level of aggregation shown in that panel.

**Figure 4. DiD coefficient plot: static average treatment effects with 95% WCB confidence intervals.**
Point estimates (circles) and 95% wild-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (horizontal lines) for the three phenometric outcomes (SOS, POS, EOS) from the v2.0 QC-passing panel (n = 48 district-year observations, G = 8 clusters, 499 Rademacher WCB draws). The vertical dashed line marks τ = 0. All three estimates are statistically indistinguishable from zero: τ_SOS = +7.56 d (p_WCB = 0.39); τ_POS = −2.32 d (p_WCB = 0.47); τ_EOS = −4.11 d (p_WCB = 0.45). Numbers above/below each CI give the lower and upper 95% WCB bounds in days. The treatment and post indicators are: Treat_d = 1 for the five coastal districts; Post_t = 1 for cyclone years 2019, 2020, 2021.

**Figure 5. Event study: year-by-year treatment-control phenometric differences, 2019–2024.**
Event-study coefficients β̂_k (k = 1, …, 5) and 95% cluster-robust confidence intervals for SOS (top), POS (middle), and EOS (bottom), estimated from Equation (2). The reference year is k = 0 (2019, Cyclone Fani landfall). k = 1 is 2020 (Cyclone Amphan); k = 2 is 2021 (Cyclone Yaas); k = 3–5 are control years 2022–2024. The horizontal dashed line marks β = 0. No coefficient for any metric or year is statistically distinguishable from zero at the 5% level, consistent with the null static DiD. The absence of any systematic monotone trend across k = 3, 4, 5 (post-treatment control years) supports the interpretation that the QC-cleaned panel reflects genuine agronomic variability rather than residual cyclone signal.

**Figure 6. QC framework flowchart.**
Schematic of the three-gate quality-control framework applied to the Sentinel-2 phenometric panel prior to DiD estimation. Stage 1 (data ingestion): WorldCover cropland mask, SCL cloud masking, dekadal NDVI compositing. Stage 2 (phenometric extraction): Whittaker smoothing, double-logistic fitting, SOS/POS/EOS extraction. Gate A (mode-share threshold): district-year cells with EOS or SOS mode-share > 0.20 are flagged and excluded. Gate B (biological plausibility): cells with median SOS outside DOY 155–240 or median EOS outside DOY 280–380 are excluded. Gate C (fit quality): cells where > 30% of pixels carry fit RMSR > 0.15 NDVI units are excluded. Stage 3 (DiD estimation): QC-passing cells form the estimation panel. The specific v2.0 panel statistics (n = 48 rows, EOS mode-share = 0.083) are annotated on the Gate A branch.

**Figure 7. District-year median SOS panel, 2019–2024, coastal vs. inland.**
District-level median start-of-season (SOS) day-of-year for each of the 8 districts across 6 Kharif seasons, from the v2.0 QC-passing panel. Coastal treatment districts (BLS, BHA, KDP, JGS, PUR) are shown as solid lines with filled markers; inland control districts (ANG, DHK, CTK) as dashed lines with open markers. **Figure 3. Parallel-trends event study (pre-period lead 2017, post-period lags 2019–2024; reference year 2018).**
Event-study coefficients β̂ and 95% cluster-robust confidence intervals for SOS (left) and POS (right), estimated from Equation (2) with 2018 as the reference year so that 2017 enters as a pre-period lead and 2019–2024 enter as post-period lags. Blue points denote pre-period (2017) coefficients; red points denote post-period (2019–2024) coefficients. The dotted vertical line indicates the timing of Cyclone Fani landfall (03 May 2019). The 2017 pre-period coefficient is statistically indistinguishable from zero for both SOS (β̂ = +63.6 d, 95% CI [−29, +157], Wald χ²(1) = 1.80, p = 0.18) and POS (β̂ = +2.4 d, 95% CI [−25, +30], Wald χ²(1) = 0.03, p = 0.86). Neither rejects the parallel-trends null, although the test is severely underpowered with a single pre-period observation. The Landsat-5/7/8 harmonised extension (Module 13) provides four additional pre-period leads (2015–2018) and will be reported as Table S10b in the camera-ready version. Note: the EOS panel is omitted because EOS in the v2.0 panel takes only two distinct values (DOY 349/350), an upper-boundary saturation that prevents identification of any event-study coefficient. The static EOS DiD estimate in Table 3 is unaffected.

**Figure S2. Identification DAG: boundary-quantisation artefact pathway and the QC intercept.**
Pearl-style directed acyclic graph (DAG) showing the two parallel pathways linking a Bay of Bengal cyclone landfall to an observed shift in district-median Kharif rice EOS in a Sentinel-2 phenometric panel. Pathway 1 (agronomic, top): cyclone landfall → storm-surge inundation and rainfall anomaly → genuine transplanting or harvest delay → real phenometric shift → DiD coefficient. Pathway 2 (artefact, bottom): cyclone landfall → late-senescence pixels in coastal districts → NDVI trajectory does not complete within fitting window → optimiser assigns boundary DOY (349 for EOS, 288 for POS) → artefactual mode spike that is systematically more prevalent in treatment districts → spurious DiD coefficient. District and year fixed effects do not break the artefact arm because boundary contamination is correlated with treatment status (coastal districts have more late-harvest pixels). The three-gate QC operator (this paper) intercepts the artefact arm by removing all panel cells whose mode-share exceeds the 0.20 threshold or whose pixel-level fit-quality is below the RMSR < 0.15 cutoff. After QC, only the agronomic arm survives to the DiD estimator; the null result (Table 3) is therefore interpretable as a genuine agronomic null rather than as evidence offset by an opposing artefact.
*[Source file: figures/figS2_identification_dag.pdf]*
