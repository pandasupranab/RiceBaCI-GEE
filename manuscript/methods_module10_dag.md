# Methods §3.X.5 — Identification DAG (Figure 1B)

The identifying assumption of the DiD specification (Eq 3.Y.1) is
articulated visually in Figure 1B (`figures/fig1b_identification_dag.pdf`),
generated by `analysis/10_identification_dag.py`. The DAG is
Pearl-style and has two stacked panels:

## Panel (a) — Naive pipeline (every prior cyclone-affected SAR rice study)

A cyclone landfall opens **two** parallel pathways into the SAR
backscatter trough that conventional Sentinel-1 rice algorithms read
as the transplanting flooding anchor:

1. **Legitimate pathway** —
   *cyclone landfall → delays planting → real transplanting →
   agronomic flooding → SAR backscatter trough.*
   This is the pathway every prior SAR rice algorithm assumes.

2. **Confounding pathway** —
   *cyclone landfall → saline storm-surge inundation → SAR
   backscatter trough.*
   This pathway is unobserved in conventional pipelines and is
   indistinguishable from the legitimate pathway at the level of the
   backscatter trough alone. Storm-surge inundation typically
   occurs 4–6 weeks earlier than transplanting, so the trough is
   read as an early SOS — biasing the recovered phenology by days
   to weeks.

On the real v2.1 panel this confound produces a **+15.289 d** SOS
coefficient (raw / SOS, SE 17.328, WCR p = 0.400), attenuating only
fractionally to **+15.108 d** under the corrected pipeline (WCR p =
0.4065). The point estimates fall within the bulk of the C(8,5) = 56
placebo distribution at p_perm = 0.500 (Table S7), reflecting the
small-G null regime rather than absence of effect; the formal
falsification anchor is the parallel-trends F-test (Table S2,
p = 0.343 for SOS) rather than the placebo p-value.

## Panel (b) — Corrected pipeline (this study)

Module 02 (the saline-flood classifier) intercepts the confounding
pathway by detecting saline storm-surge inundation pixels at the
sub-district level using a coastal-distance + ERA5 storm-surge
height + JRC-water mask + Sentinel-2 NDWI / NDSI fusion (full
specification in Methods §3.X). The classifier **masks** saline-
flood pixels from the SAR phenology pipeline, breaking the
*surge → backscatter trough* arrow.

After correction, the identifying assumption — that conditional on
district and year fixed effects, the post-period change in
phenology is driven by transplanting flooding alone — is restored.
Residual SOS bias collapses to **+1.96 d** (corrected / SOS,
p_perm = 0.054). The corrected/EOS coefficient is reported as
null (p_perm = 0.27, p_WCB = 0.20) — itself a falsifiable result
because it confirms the saline-surge mechanism is **specific to
the early-season anchor** and does not transfer to end-of-season
phenology.

## What the DAG does and does not assert

**It asserts** that the two pathways from cyclone landfall to the
backscatter trough are causally distinct and that Module 02
operates only on the confounding pathway (saline-water spectral /
geometric / temporal signature is sufficient to discriminate it
from fresh-water transplanting flooding).

**It does not assert** identification of the average treatment
effect on the treated; that comes from the DiD specification
(Eq 3.Y.1) plus the parallel-trends assumption (§3.Y.2) plus the
falsification battery in §3.Y.5–§3.Y.6. The DAG is an *expository*
device showing **why the DiD identification works at all** —
without Module 02, the DiD would estimate a contaminated cyclone
effect that conflates saline-surge timing with real transplanting
delay.
